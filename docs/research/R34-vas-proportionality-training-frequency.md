# R34 — Is the VAS proportionality instruction given at the first use of each scale in every session, or only in the participant's first session?

**Asked:** 2026-09-25. **For:** `docs/SPEC.md` §10.6 (VAS training — proportionality), with §2 (session outline) and §16 (blinding). **Confidence:** medium

No source was found that tests how often a ratio-use instruction should be repeated. The recommendation rests on the spec's own session template, on the general rule that a crossover keeps its periods procedurally identical, and on evidence that scale use drifts between days. Price et al. 1983 and 1994 could be read only as abstracts. Bilaga 1 §3.6.1 could not be read, because the ethics folder is outside the repository and was not available to this agent (see "What would change this").

## The question

`docs/SPEC.md` §10.6 says the proportionality training ("a mark twice as far along the line means it was twice as painful", with the same form for touch intensity and pleasantness) is "given once per scale at first use". It does not say whether "first use" means first use by the participant (session 1 only) or first use in each session (all three sessions). The comment in `config/text/participant_{en,sv}.yaml` under `training:` repeats the same ambiguous phrase. The choice affects what is measured, because the instruction is meant to shape how the participant uses the line, and every rating in the study is taken on that line.

What the spec does fix:

- **§2 describes one session, and it runs three times.** In that session template, "VAS familiarisation" is part of touch calibration and "VAS training" is part of pre-sensitisation. Neither is marked as happening in session 1 only. Where the spec does mean session 1 only, it says so: §8.2 step 1 starts the pinprick search from "a configured default at pre-S of session 1".
- **Precedent within the spec.** Two other participant procedures were deliberately made per-session. The stop rehearsal runs every session "because a participant who last pressed it four weeks ago has not rehearsed it" (§9, stop rehearsal). Preference selection runs "at the start of every session, not only in the sessions where it is used, so its presence is not a tell" (§16, §9 step 6).
- **§16 requires only that nothing differs by condition.** Both options meet that, because neither depends on the condition. The "session 1 only" option depends on session number, which is not the condition.

So the spec leans towards "every session", but does not state it.

## What the evidence says

1. **Price et al. did not give participants a ratio instruction.** They showed that VAS ratings behave as a ratio scale without being told to. In Price et al. 1983, heat-pain VAS ratings followed power functions, and those functions predicted separately obtained ratio judgements of pairs of temperatures, which the authors took as "direct evidence for ratio scaling properties of VAS" (Price, McGrath, Rafii & Buckingham 1983, *Pain* 17:45–56, [doi:10.1016/0304-3959(83)90126-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3959(83)90126-4); abstract via [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/6226917)). Price et al. 1994 took the same approach: the sensory M-VAS power function "predicted temperatures chosen as twice as intense as standard temperatures of 47 °C and 48 °C" (Price, Bush, Long & Harkins 1994, *Pain* 56:217–226, [doi:10.1016/0304-3959(94)90097-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3959(94)90097-3); abstract via [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/8008411)). Neither abstract describes an instruction telling participants that twice the distance means twice the magnitude, and neither was a multi-session design. *Read as abstracts only* (a copy of the 1983 PDF was reachable but could not be text-extracted). **Consequence:** the instruction in §10.6 is Bilaga 1's adaptation, grounded in Price's validation. Price's papers do not say how often it should be given. Whatever frequency is chosen has to be justified from design principles, not from the source.

2. **Scale use drifts between sessions on different days, and the first session tends to differ from the later ones.** Over four weekly sessions, heat pain thresholds showed "bias between first and subsequent sessions but not among later sessions", and suprathreshold VAS ratings had poor inter-session repeatability. The authors advised long-term studies to use the second or later session as the baseline (Yarnitsky, Sprecher, Zaslansky & Hemli 1996, *Pain* 67:327–333, [doi:10.1016/0304-3959(96)03110-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3959(96)03110-7); abstract via [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/8951926)). A recent three-session study on separate days found good test–retest reliability for pain-intensity ratings (ICC 0.755), but self-reported pain "varied significantly across sessions". Of the measures, "only secondary hyperalgesia area was stable across sessions", and the authors recommend counterbalancing sessions (De Vera, Abssy, Seminowicz & Moayedi 2026, *J Pain*, [doi:10.1016/j.jpain.2026.106253](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpain.2026.106253), [PubMed 41765342](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41765342/); *abstract only*). This design is close to TATP's: three sessions, pain ratings and secondary hyperalgesia.

3. **Individual scaling behaviour carries over from day to day only partly, and a gap of one week can erase the carry-over.** In magnitude-estimation work, the correlation between one person's exponents across sessions fell to near zero after a week for some continua (visual size, line length matched to brightness) and survived for loudness. The authors interpret the session-to-session consistency as having a *mnemonic* basis that the passage of time alone can disrupt (Teghtsoonian & Teghtsoonian 1983, *Percept Psychophys* 33:203–214, [doi:10.3758/BF03202857](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03202857); *read only as a search-engine abstract snippet, and attribution between this paper and their 1971 note, [doi:10.3758/BF03205774](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03205774), should be confirmed*). **Relevance:** an instruction given only in session 1 would rely on the participant remembering how they used the line days or weeks earlier. Repeating it each session re-establishes the rule instead of relying on that memory.

4. **Crossover design: periods should be procedurally identical, because period effects exist and are only balanced out approximately.** TATP allocates conditions in a counterbalanced order (§2). The counterbalancing file is for 41 participants (§20 item 10). Forty-one is not a multiple of 3 or 6, so no complete Latin-square or all-orders scheme balances session position exactly across conditions, and dropouts can unbalance it further. Findings 2 and 3 show that session effects exist anyway. A procedural difference that is present only in session 1 adds a deliberate period effect on top of them. With imperfect balance, part of that effect leaks into the condition contrast. With good balance, it still adds variance. This is standard crossover reasoning rather than a finding from one paper. De Vera et al. 2026 (finding 2) reach the same practical recommendation for pain ratings.

5. **Practice in published protocols.** No multi-session pain study reached in the time available states explicitly whether a ratio-use instruction was repeated each session. Standardised-script designs, such as the DFNS QST protocol (Rolke et al. 2006, *Eur J Pain* 10:77–88, [doi:10.1016/j.ejpain.2005.02.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejpain.2005.02.003)) and its multi-session reliability study (Geber et al. 2011, *Pain* 152:548–556, [doi:10.1016/j.pain.2010.11.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pain.2010.11.013)), deliver their instructions as part of each test administration. However, I could not read either full text to confirm what is said at each visit. **Not relied on for the recommendation.**

## Options

**A. Every session, at the first use of each scale in that session.** The pain instruction comes at the first pain rating of each session (pre-sensitisation VAS training). The intensity and pleasantness instructions come at the first touch-intensity and touch-pleasantness rating of each session (touch-calibration VAS familiarisation, which precedes pre-sensitisation in §2).
- *Cost:* three short screens per session, a few seconds each. A returning participant may find the reminder redundant.
- *Risk to measurement validity:* low. All three sessions are procedurally identical, so the instruction is a constant of the session template, not a period effect. The one residual difference is that the instruction is new in session 1 and familiar later. That novelty effect cannot be removed by either option, and the counterbalanced order handles it.
- *Blinding:* the instruction is identical in every session and every condition. It depends on nothing, so it is trivially condition-independent.
- *Software:* stateless. The session does not need to know whether an earlier session happened or completed. That is robust to a session 1 that was aborted and rerun, or to sessions run out of order.

**B. Session 1 only, at first use of each scale.**
- *Cost:* saves a few seconds in sessions 2 and 3.
- *Risk to measurement validity:* moderate. It introduces a deliberate procedural difference confined to session 1, on top of the known first-session effect (finding 2). It relies on the participant retaining the ratio rule across days to weeks (finding 3). With 41 participants the difference cannot be exactly balanced across conditions (finding 4). If the instruction changes how people use the line, which is its purpose, then scale use in sessions 2 and 3 will depend on how well each participant remembered it.
- *Blinding:* not a condition tell, since the order is counterbalanced. It does make session 1 look different to the experimenter, which is harmless but unnecessary.
- *Software:* requires cross-session state: which sessions have run, and whether the training in them completed. That brings a new failure mode, and a question about what to do when session 1 was aborted.

**C. Full instruction in session 1, shorter reminder in sessions 2 and 3.** Rejected. Two versions of the instruction mean two different stimuli to the participant. That brings back option B's period difference, and it needs a second wording that has not been approved.

## Recommendation

**Option A: give the proportionality instruction at the first use of each scale in every session.** It keeps the three sessions procedurally identical, which is what a counterbalanced within-subject crossover depends on. It does not rely on participants remembering scale use across days. It matches the §2 session template, which lists VAS training and familiarisation in every session, and it follows the reasoning the spec already uses for the stop rehearsal and preference selection. It is condition-independent by construction, so it cannot be a blinding leak. It costs a few seconds per scale per session.

For the main session: "first use" should be read as the first rating on that scale *within the session*. The config comment "given once per scale at first use" could then be made explicit ("once per scale per session, at its first use"). That is the main session's edit to make, not this report's.

## What would change this

- **Bilaga 1 §3.6.1 (or the application) states the frequency.** If the approved research plan says the training is given only at the first visit, or only at inclusion, then that is what the application promised. Either follow it, or record the change as a deviation in `docs/LOG.md` alongside the two already listed in §10.6. The main session should check with `make ethics ARGS="Bilaga1_Forskningsplan_V2.docx --grep twice --context 4"` before implementing. This agent could not reach the ethics folder.
- **Pilot evidence that the repeated instruction shifts ratings in sessions 2–3 relative to session 1.** For example, a jump in the pre-sensitisation pinprick ratings at the same filament force, larger than the session-to-session drift seen without the instruction. That would suggest the instruction acts as a re-anchoring event rather than a neutral reminder. Even then it would argue for keeping it identical everywhere, not for dropping it after session 1.
- **Full text of Price et al. 1983/1994** showing that participants *were* given an explicit ratio instruction, and on what schedule, would give a direct precedent in place of the design-principle argument.
